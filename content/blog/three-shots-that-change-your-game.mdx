Almost everyone arrives at padel from tennis, and almost everyone spends their
first month playing tennis on a smaller court. These are the three habits that
break that, in the order they usually click.

## 1. Let the ball come off the glass

The instinct is to take the ball before it reaches the wall. Resist it. Turn
side-on, let the ball pass you, and hit it as it comes back off the glass — it
arrives slower, sitting up, at a comfortable height. The wall is not an obstacle
you are avoiding; it is a teammate that returns the ball to you politely.

If you only change one thing, change this one. It is the difference between
defending badly and defending well.

## 2. Lob on purpose, not in panic

In padel the lob is not a last resort, it is the main way to take the net away
from the pair that has it. Aim deep, over the outside shoulder of the player at
the net, and follow it forward.

A good lob wins more points at club level than a good smash does. It is also far
easier to learn.

## 3. Move to the net together

The most common mistake in a mixed-ability pair is one player charging forward
while their partner stays back. That diagonal gap is where every decent opponent
will hit.

Move up as a pair, retreat as a pair. Say "up" or "back" out loud — it feels
awkward for two games and then it feels normal.
